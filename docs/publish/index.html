The road condition signal does not clear its own bar

The World Bank puts the paved share of Nigeria's road network at 30 per cent. Above are the two ends of that range: a Lagos street where buses and vans pick their way through standing water, and a dual carriageway outside Abuja, empty, lit and drained.

A photograph shows which of the two a road is on the day it was taken. It does not show which way the road is heading. The flooded street may flood every rainy season and be no worse than ten years ago; the carriageway may be losing its verge now.

In 2017 the World Bank put 40 per cent of federal roads in poor condition. That is one year's reading, not a trend. This page measures movement instead, over 850 federal corridors and 18,419 km of them: not which roads look bad today, but which are getting worse.

What the record says

Nigeria's procurement record is the only national paper trail of road spending. It names a road, a state, a kind of work, an amount and a date: money allocated.

It does not say whether the road got better. A contract records a promise, and a commissioning ceremony is a photograph of a promise, nothing more. The money may have reached the road, part of it, or none of it.

The record shows where money moved; satellite imagery shows what the ground did. The test below asks whether the second predicts the first.

The result is not "the effect is small". It is that the instrument ranks the labelled corridors the wrong way round, so it cannot be used to allocate maintenance, and this page exists to say so before anything else on it.

The five figures

How the measurement was made

For each corridor and each month, the mean vegetation index of a band along the carriageway is subtracted from the mean of the verge beside it, at 10 metre resolution, from Sentinel-2 via the DE Africa platform. That gives one value per corridor per month: 18,700 corridor-months over 22 months, with 183 left blank rather than filled.

The test asks whether the contrast falls faster on corridors with a procurement record than on comparable corridors without one. Corridors are matched exactly on road class and on length band within class; the statistic is the probability that a labelled corridor ranks as more degraded than an unlabelled one, with a bootstrap interval and a permutation null. The bar was written down before the test ran, and was not lowered after it failed.

What this does not claim

Four things this does not claim.

  1. Movement, not condition. The score has no level: a corridor that was always bad and one that never changed can produce the same value.
  2. Federal corridors only. The network is the named federal highways; feeder roads, which carry much of the traffic, are not in it.
  3. Reanalysis, not gauges. The rainfall covariate is a gridded reanalysis product, not a rain gauge, and it is context rather than a measured cause.
  4. Observational, not causal. 66 labelled corridors is a small, non-random, spending-driven sample. A procurement record is evidence that money was allocated, never that a road failed.
